Reference

Words used on this page

Edge
How much better a signal did than other stocks bought the same morning and sold the same day. It removes the whole market's move.
t-stat
How sure we can be an edge isn't luck. About 2 or more is the usual bar; under 1 is indistinguishable from chance.
In-sample / out-of-sample
Rules are picked on one period and judged on another they never saw. Real patterns hold up in both.
Pre-registered
The rules and pass marks were committed to git before the test ran, so they couldn't be adjusted after seeing results.
bps
Basis points, hundredths of a percent. 5 bps per side = 0.05% lost on each buy and each sell.
CAGR, Sharpe, worst fall
Average yearly return; return per unit of risk above T-bills (higher is better); largest drop from a peak.

Buy every stock that drops 2% in a day

Idea

Test

S&P 500 and 400 stocks, 2005–Oct 2026. Down 2%+, 1M+ shares, price over $20. Buy at the next open, sell 1–5 days later.

Test 5 of 6 · first portfolio review

Regime-gated, risk-budgeted momentum

Idea

Replace concentrated chip trades with a monthly portfolio: top 20 stocks by 12-1 month momentum, max 5 per sector, inverse-volatility sizing, 15% volatility target, cash when SPY is below its 200-day average, sell all at a 20% loss from peak.

Test

Pre-registered; 5 and 10 bps per side; cash earns T-bills; three samples; ablations A–D.

Finding

Smaller worst falls everywhere, but lower Sharpe than SPY in 2 of 3 samples. Momentum picking added nothing beyond the market switch.

Test 6 of 6 · second portfolio review

Put-write income layer on idle cash

Idea

Use half the account (cash-secured, no leverage) to sell 1-month at-the-money S&P 500 puts each month, collecting the premium option buyers pay for crash insurance.

Test

Pre-registered on the CBOE PutWrite index (^PUT, real data from 1996). Split at the index launch (2007). Assumed 0.5%/yr costs and US taxes.

Finding

VIX beat later realized volatility on 81%–86% of days, but after costs and annual taxes the layer didn't beat SPY or a 50/50 mix per unit of risk after 2007.

Can you actually trade it?

  • XYLD, the closest liquid fund (buy-write, from 2013): 8.2% a year, Sharpe 0.59, vs SPY 14.2% and 0.85 (pre-tax).

Verdict: failed. Keep idle cash in T-bills rather than adding an options layer.
Summary

Current recommendation

Hold. No new trades. None of the six ideas cleared its pre-registered test. Keep idle cash earning the T-bill rate (about 4.0%).

Risks to review

  • The stock sleeve is concentrated in chips/tech, with beta 2.15 to the S&P 500, into chip earnings and a likely December rate hike.
  • The SIGNUM bot logged no activity after 2026-10-14; check that its routine is running.

Lessons

  • Measure signals against stocks bought the same day, or market bounces look like skill.
  • Search enough rules and one will look great by chance; only unseen data tells.
  • Costs and taxes decide short-term and options strategies.
  • Lead for a future pre-registered test: SPY with a 200-day switch cut worst falls by more than half in every window, but lagged in the 2016–26 bull market.

How it was tested

  • Daily prices from Yahoo Finance (adjusted for splits and dividends); index membership from Wikipedia; T-bill yield ^IRX.
  • Trades buy at the next open after a signal; costs charged on traded value; cash earns T-bills where stated.
  • Tests 2, 4, 5 and 6 were committed to git before running. Stocks that went bankrupt or were acquired are missing from the data, which flatters stock-picking results.
